What matters for a court in Brigham City

Cache Valley has some of the coldest winters along the Wasatch Front, with long stretches below freezing and plenty of snow. Courts here need a well-drained base and a coating system that can handle the slab moving with the cold.

Summers are shorter but warm and sunny, so the best building window is late spring through early fall. Booking early helps, because the season goes fast.

Tips before you build

  • Book in winter or early spring to get on the schedule for the short building season.
  • Make sure water drains away from the court and doesn't refreeze on it.
  • Consider a lighter court color if you want the surface to stay cooler in summer.

Brigham City court FAQ

Does Ace Coatings work in Brigham City?

Yes. Brigham City is part of our Cache Valley and Box Elder County service area. We're based in Spanish Fork and build courts all over Utah, and estimates are free.

How long does a court take to build in Brigham City?

Resurfacing an existing slab usually takes a few days of good weather. A new court takes longer because fresh concrete needs to cure, often a few weeks, before it can be coated. We'll give you a real timeline at the estimate.

Do I need a permit for a backyard court in Brigham City?

It depends on the size of the slab, fences, and lights. Rules vary by city and HOA, so check with Brigham City's building or planning office and your HOA before you build. We can tell you what other homeowners usually need.

Can you resurface my existing court or slab?

Usually, yes. If the concrete is solid and drains well, we repair cracks, prep the surface, and put down a new acrylic coating and fresh lines. If the slab has bigger problems, we'll tell you honestly.

Can one court have pickleball and basketball?

Yes. Multi-use courts are one of the most popular things we build. We can lay out pickleball lines with a basketball key, and add a hoop, nets, and lights.
