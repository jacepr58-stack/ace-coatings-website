What matters for a court in North Ogden

Northern Utah winters are long, with more snow and more freeze-thaw nights than the south end of the state. That's hard on concrete, so we put the effort into the base, the cure, and flexible acrylic coatings that move with the slab.

Bench lots below the mountains are often sloped and may need grading or a retaining edge to get a flat court. Valley lots toward West Haven and Roy are flatter and usually simpler to build on.

Tips before you build

  • Use a plastic shovel to clear snow. Metal blades scrape coatings.
  • Plan the slab with a slight slope so meltwater runs off.
  • Ask about lights if you want to keep playing on short winter evenings.

North Ogden court FAQ

Does Ace Coatings work in North Ogden?

Yes. North Ogden is part of our Weber County service area. We're based in Spanish Fork and build courts all over Utah, and estimates are free.

What does a court cost in North Ogden?

Price depends on size, whether you need new concrete, site prep, and add-ons like hoops, fences, and lights. Every yard is different, so we give free estimates based on your site instead of a one-size-fits-all number.

How long does a court take to build in North Ogden?

Resurfacing an existing slab usually takes a few days of good weather. A new court takes longer because fresh concrete needs to cure, often a few weeks, before it can be coated. We'll give you a real timeline at the estimate.

Do I need a permit for a backyard court in North Ogden?

It depends on the size of the slab, fences, and lights. Rules vary by city and HOA, so check with North Ogden's building or planning office and your HOA before you build. We can tell you what other homeowners usually need.

Can you resurface my existing court or slab?

Usually, yes. If the concrete is solid and drains well, we repair cracks, prep the surface, and put down a new acrylic coating and fresh lines. If the slab has bigger problems, we'll tell you honestly.
