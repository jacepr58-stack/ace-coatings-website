Built for Southern Utah

What matters for a court in Washington

St. George, Washington, and Hurricane see summer highs well over 100°F and intense sun almost year round. Heat and UV are the big enemies there, so color choice, UV-stable coatings, and good surface texture matter most.

Cedar City sits much higher and gets real winters with freeze-thaw cycles, more like northern Utah. The same court needs a different focus depending on which side of the hill it's on, and we plan for that.

Tips before you build

  • Lighter colors stay cooler to the touch in desert heat.
  • In St. George, plan around summer heat. Coatings go on best in milder weather, so spring and fall fill up first.
  • Ask about lights. A lot of Southern Utah play happens after the sun goes down.

Questions

Washington court FAQ

Does Ace Coatings work in Washington?

Yes. Washington is part of our Southern Utah service area. We're based in Spanish Fork and build courts all over Utah, and estimates are free.

Do I need a permit for a backyard court in Washington?

It depends on the size of the slab, fences, and lights. Rules vary by city and HOA, so check with Washington's building or planning office and your HOA before you build. We can tell you what other homeowners usually need.

Can you resurface my existing court or slab?

Usually, yes. If the concrete is solid and drains well, we repair cracks, prep the surface, and put down a new acrylic coating and fresh lines. If the slab has bigger problems, we'll tell you honestly.

Can one court have pickleball and basketball?

Yes. Multi-use courts are one of the most popular things we build. We can lay out pickleball lines with a basketball key, and add a hoop, nets, and lights.

What does a court cost in Washington?

Price depends on size, whether you need new concrete, site prep, and add-ons like hoops, fences, and lights. Every yard is different, so we give free estimates based on your site instead of a one-size-fits-all number.
