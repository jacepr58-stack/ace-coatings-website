How Much Does It Cost to Build a Basketball Court?

Thinking about building your own basketball court is exciting—no more waiting for an open hoop, just step outside and play. But before you start sketching three-point lines, you need a clear idea of how much it will cost and what drives those numbers up or down.

Typical Cost Range for a Basketball Court

The question most people start with is simple: how much does it cost to build a basketball court? Current national data from sources like Forbes, HomeAdvisor, and Fixr shows that:

  • Outdoor courts typically range from about $15,000 to $45,000, with many homeowners spending around $30,000–$35,000.
  • Full-size outdoor courts can climb higher, commonly $35,000 to $75,000 depending on site work and surface quality.
  • Indoor courts are a major investment, often ranging from about $150,000 to $500,000 for a full-size court once you factor in the building, flooring, and systems.

These numbers are broad, but they give you a realistic starting point. Your final price will depend on size, materials, and how “pro-level” you want the court to feel.

Full Court vs. Half Court: How Size Impacts Price

One of the biggest cost drivers is simply how much playing surface you build. A regulation NBA court is 94 by 50 feet, while many homeowners opt for a slightly smaller full court or a half court to fit their yard and budget.

  • Half court (most popular for homes): Often falls in the $15,000–$30,000 range outdoors, depending on base prep and surface choice.
  • Full court: Usually starts around $30,000–$40,000 outdoors and can exceed $75,000 with premium finishes and extras.

If you’re on the fence, remember that a well-designed half court still allows for shooting, drills, and small-sided games while cutting costs significantly.

Breaking Down the Major Cost Components

1. Site Preparation and Base

Before you pour or coat anything, the ground needs to be properly graded, compacted, and prepared. This can range from a few thousand dollars for a relatively flat, accessible yard to significantly more if you need excavation, drainage, or retaining walls. A solid base is crucial for a long-lasting, crack-resistant court.

2. Playing Surface Material

Outdoor courts are typically built with concrete or asphalt, then finished with a sports coating system for traction, color, and UV protection. Higher-end acrylic or cushioned systems add comfort and performance but also increase the price. Indoor courts usually feature hardwood or synthetic sports flooring, which is why they cost much more overall.

3. Hoops, Lines, and Accessories

Quality, adjustable hoop systems can run from a few hundred dollars to several thousand each. Professional line striping, logos, and custom colors also add to the final bill but make a huge difference in how the court looks and plays. Don’t forget optional items like fencing, ball nets, and storage.

High-quality coatings improve traction, protect concrete, and extend your court’s lifespan.

Hidden and Long-Term Costs to Keep in Mind

  • Permits and approvals: Some areas require permits or HOA approval for large slabs or lighting. Fees vary but should be part of your budget.
  • Maintenance: Plan for periodic cleaning, crack repair, and recoating every several years, especially on outdoor courts.
  • Upgrades over time: You might start with a half court and later add lighting, fencing, or a second hoop as your needs and budget grow.

How to Get an Accurate Quote for Your Basketball Court

Online averages are helpful, but the only way to know what your court will cost is to talk with a contractor who can see your space, understand your goals, and account for local material and labor prices. They’ll look at access for equipment, drainage, slope, and any obstacles that might affect the design or price.

Is Building a Basketball Court Worth the Investment?

For many families and basketball enthusiasts, the answer is yes. A well-built court adds daily convenience, encourages active lifestyles, and can become a social hub for friends and neighbors. While the upfront cost is significant, the years of use you’ll get—without driving to a gym or park—often make it a smart long-term investment, especially when the court is designed and finished to last.

By understanding the main cost factors, planning the right size, and working with experienced professionals, you can create a basketball court that fits your budget today and keeps you playing for many seasons to come.
