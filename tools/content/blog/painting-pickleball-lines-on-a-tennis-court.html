Understanding the Basics of Painting Pickleball Lines on a Tennis Court

Before diving into the process, it’s important to know the required dimensions and layout. A standard pickleball court measures 20 feet wide by 44 feet long for both singles and doubles play. The kitchen (or non-volley zone) extends 7 feet from the net on both sides. When painting pickleball lines on a tennis court, maintaining accurate measurements ensures the court remains playable and fair for all players.

For official measurements, refer to USA Pickleball’s official court specifications. Following these guidelines helps ensure your court meets standard play conditions and enhances its long-term value.

Step 1: Preparing the Surface

Proper preparation is the foundation of a clean finish. Start by thoroughly cleaning the court to remove debris, dirt, and loose coatings. Pressure washing is ideal for removing stubborn buildup. Allow the surface to dry completely before moving on to taping and line marking.

If there are cracks or uneven areas, they should be repaired using an acrylic patching compound or crack filler. In Utah’s climate, where freeze-thaw cycles are common, surface prep ensures your lines stay crisp and don’t peel prematurely.

Step 2: Measuring and Marking Pickleball Lines

Precision marking is the most important part of the project. Use high-quality masking tape to outline the boundaries based on pickleball dimensions. You can fit two pickleball courts on one tennis court, or just one if space is limited.

The keyphrase painting pickleball lines on a tennis court means getting these markings exact — even minor inaccuracies can affect gameplay. Double-check that the service boxes, non-volley zones, and baselines align properly before painting begins.

Step 3: Choosing the Right Paint and Tools

Always use acrylic-based court coating paints designed for outdoor sports surfaces. These are UV-resistant, quick-drying, and flexible enough to withstand Utah’s weather variations. A small roller or airless sprayer provides even coverage without overspray.

When painting pickleball lines on a tennis court, use contrasting colors so the new pickleball boundaries stand out clearly against the tennis lines — typically white or yellow for pickleball. Two coats are recommended for best durability and visibility.

Step 4: Applying Paint and Removing Tape

Once the paint is applied evenly, allow at least 24 hours of curing time before removing the tape. Pull the tape at a 45-degree angle for crisp, clean edges. Avoid foot traffic on the newly painted lines until they are fully cured.

This step ensures your court lines stay sharp and don’t tear or blur. Professionals at Ace Coatings use precision tools and industrial-grade paints for this stage to guarantee a flawless finish.

Step 5: Final Inspection and Maintenance

After painting, inspect the lines to confirm uniform thickness and straightness. Any missed spots can be touched up using a small brush. To maintain your court’s fresh look, schedule regular cleanings and re-coating every few years.

Proper maintenance not only extends your court’s lifespan but also keeps it safe and visually appealing for players.
